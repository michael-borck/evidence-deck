The Evidence Deck · Desk card

Every card. Every line.

The whole deck on one page. Pin it up; the lines do the remembering.

01Claims

Policy versus control — A policy is a claim. A control is a claim that has survived testing.

The board does not need a policy — The board does not need a policy. It needs to know whether the policy is true.

The demo versus the deployment — A demo is engineered to be impressive. A deployment has to work.

Fluency is camouflage — The most dangerous AI output is the one nobody checked.

Agreement is not evidence — Agreement is not evidence.

02Gates

Gate one: prototype to bounded pilot — A polished prototype is not a production system.

Gate two: pilot to production — Do not quietly widen the scope.

Gate three: continued operation — Is it still working — and how would you know if it stopped?

Re-baselining — A changed threshold is a new decision to explain.

The dangerous practitioner — Name the line before you ship.

03Measurement

Headline accuracy is an assertion — Headline accuracy is an assertion, not evidence.

A model without a threshold — A model without a stated threshold is a demo, not an evaluation.

The worst-group gap — The worst-group gap is the honest headline.

Adversarial testing — Break it on purpose, before someone else does it for you.

Small samples — A sample that cannot support the claim is an anecdote with a date.

04Ratings

The defensible Partial — A defensible Partial is worth more than an unsupported Met.

Triangulation — If they disagree, the disagreement is the finding.

The limitation beside the rating — Limitations belong beside the rating.

Refusing to rate — A refusal to rate is a finding.

05Records

The proof brief, before building — Start the brief now. Don't reconstruct the reasoning after the results.

The dossier — The product is a defensible judgement.

The conversation log — A demo persuades; a log proves.

The threshold sentence — For this decision, good enough means this, because of this.

Prove, don't persuade

A demo persuades; a log proves. The log shows where the AI was confidently wrong and you caught it.

The defensible Partial

A Partial with a clear limitation gives the board a decision. A Met with no evidence gives comfort that has not been earned.

The three gates

Prototype, pilot, continued operation — each gate has its own evidence, and the dangerous practitioner cannot see the line.
